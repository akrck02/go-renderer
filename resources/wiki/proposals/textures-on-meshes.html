2. Textures on meshes

Status: done · Author’s approval: proposal ☑ (2026-10-26, with the decisions below) plan ☑ (2026-10-26, “Dale, implementa”)

Why

A 3D surface in Go Renderer has one colour: its material’s BaseColor times its vertex colours, plus six procedural patterns (cobbles, masonry, plaster, shingles, planks, grass) that only the desktop’s shaders draw. A game that shows more needs pictures on its surfaces:

  • Tiling detail: panels, a grid, stripes, carbon weave, hexagons, wear, camouflage, windows — small pictures repeated across a part and tinted by its colour.
  • Pictures made at run time: a game generates a pattern, or downloads one, and gives it to many meshes at once.
  • Models that carry their own: a .glb file’s textures are read and thrown away today.
  • The same on every target, the 3DS included, within its limits.

Today scene.Mesh has no texture coordinates and scene.Material no texture, on any backend.

What

Id Requirement Check
R1 A mesh built from code carries texture coordinates (UVs, two per vertex) test: a mesh with UVs uploads and draws; a mesh without them draws as today
R2 A material can carry a texture: the surface’s colour is BaseColor × vertex colour × the texture, lit as before frames compared under Mesa: a tinted texture, the same texture in two colours
R3 A texture repeats (or clamps, or mirrors) across the surface, with a scale and an offset on the material, so one small picture tiles a large part frames compared: a 64 px picture repeated eight times across a box
R4 A texture is made from Go pixels at run time (an image.Image, or pixels written and marked changed), kept and shared by many meshes; changing it changes every mesh that shows it, uploaded once test: one texture on a hundred meshes is uploaded once; a change uploads it again once
R5 The 3DS draws textured meshes too: the texture multiplies the baked colour, its sizes and formats are written down (powers of two from 8 to 1024 px a side; which formats), and a texture that does not fit is scaled or refused with an error code Azahar capture of a tiled, tinted part; tests of the size rules
R6 loaders.ReadScene and LoadScene read a glTF’s base colour textures and texture coordinates (TEXCOORD_0, baseColorTexture, its sampler’s wrapping) test with a small .glb that carries a texture
R7 A frame with textures allocates nothing, and a scene without textures costs what it costs today the benchmarks of the optimization plan, before and after

Out of scope

  • Normal, roughness, metallic and emissive maps: one colour texture first.
  • Texture atlases built by the renderer, and texture compression on the desktop.
  • Fetching pictures from the network: a program downloads them and hands over the pixels.

Decisions

The author’s answers (2026-10-26):

  1. Where a texture lives: a small scene.Texture that holds a draw2d.Image for its pixels (one way to keep pixels, for 2D and 3D alike) and the settings only 3D needs: wrapping, filtering, the 3DS’s format (“Me parece correcto”).
  2. Filtering: chosen per texture, smooth or sharp.
  3. The 3DS’s formats: save memory where it can: RGBA8, RGB565 and ETC1, chosen per texture.
  4. The procedural patterns: they become built-in textures, so every target, the 3DS included, draws them; the pattern code in the desktop’s shaders goes.

After building, the author answered the review’s questions (2026-10-26, “Dale”):

  1. The old glTF extras stay, deprecated: "pattern" and "patternScale" are read as the built-in texture of that name, the cell size turned into the texture scale that keeps it.
  2. The built-in textures keep a surface’s brightness: each carries a Gain (a new scene.Texture field) that brings its mean in linear light to 1; the material’s colour is multiplied by it, so nothing changes per pixel.
  3. The 3DS’s limits (no textures on water and moving nodes, no mipmaps, the fog tinted by the texture) stay as documented.
  4. go-engine follows on its branch feature/material-textures.

Design

In scene. Texture is small and kept by the program:

type Texture struct {
	Picture   *draw2d.Image // its pixels; Picture.Changed() uploads them again
	Wrap      Wrap          // Repeat (the zero value), Clamp, Mirror
	Sharp     bool          // nearest pixel instead of smooth (with mipmaps on the desktop)
	Format    TextureFormat // on the 3DS: RGBA8 (default), RGB565 or ETC1; the desktop keeps RGBA8
}

Mesh gains UVs []float32 (two per vertex, as Positions has three). Material gains Texture *Texture and TextureScale, TextureOffset ([2]float32; a zero scale is 1): the surface’s colour is BaseColor × vertex colour × the texture at (UV × scale + offset). A material without a texture draws exactly as today. A textured mesh without UVs takes them from its positions (Mesh.SurfaceUVs: x and z on what faces up, along and up on walls, one model unit a repeat), as the procedural patterns did from the world position: the island’s terrain, a box made in code and a model without TEXCOORD_0 can still wear a tiling texture.

Changed while building (2026-10-26): the plan said a mesh without UVs draws as today; the patterns it replaces need no UVs, so without the projection every pattern would have been lost on meshes that have none. The projection is worked out once, on the processor, when the mesh is uploaded: every backend, the 3DS included, gets the same UVs and no shader changes for it.

Built-in textures. scene.BuiltInTexture(name string) (*Texture, bool) gives textures made in Go the first time they are asked for, then kept: the six patterns of today (cobbles, masonry, plaster, shingles, planks, grass) and common tiling ones (panels, grid, stripes, carbon, hexagons, worn, camouflage, windows), each a small power of two that tiles without a seam. Material.Pattern and PatternScale go, with the patterns’ code in the desktop’s shaders: a material asks for BuiltInTexture("cobbles") and a TextureScale instead. Nobody outside the repository uses Pattern (go-engine and Go UI checked).

The backends. Each keeps uploaded textures by the picture’s id and version, as the 2D picture atlas does: one upload for any number of meshes, again only after Changed(); opaque draws are sorted by texture, so a texture is bound once a frame. (Changed while building: materials were never sorted, and sorting by them would buy nothing, since a material is only uniforms; the untextured draws keep their order, first.) A texture’s pixels are sRGB, as a PNG’s are: the desktop and the browser turn them into linear light as they read them.

  • OpenGL (desktop), OpenGL ES (Android and the browser), Metal (its AI-written renderers): a texCoord attribute, a sampler, wrapping and filtering as the texture says, mipmaps when smooth.
  • The 3DS: the PICA200’s texture combiner multiplies the texture by the baked vertex colour, so lighting stays baked and textures cost a texture read per pixel, no vertex work. Sizes are powers of two from 8 to 1024 a side (a larger or odd picture is scaled once, with a warning). RGB565 and ETC1 are encoded in Go when uploaded (ETC1: a small encoder, 4 bits a pixel, no alpha; a texture with transparency stays RGBA8 or is refused with an error code).

glTF. ReadScene and LoadScene read TEXCOORD_0, the base colour texture of the PBR material (PNG or JPEG, embedded in a .glb or by URI beside the file) and its sampler’s wrapping and filtering.

Alternatives not taken. Textures in draw2d.Image itself: 3D-only settings in a 2D type. A texture per mesh instead of per material: materials are already what meshes share. Lighting per pixel on the 3DS for textured meshes: the measurements show its vertex work is the cost to avoid.

Result

Built on 2026-10-26, T1 to T8, on docs/textures-on-meshes. Every requirement’s check was run:

  • R1–R4 (OpenGL): opengl/textures_test.go compares a frame of a tinted 64-pixel picture repeated eight times across two quads (smooth and red, sharp and blue) with opengl/testdata/two-tinted-textures.png (Mesa, 8 levels a channel, 0.5 % of pixels); adding the texture instead of multiplying it fails it (58.6 % of pixels differ). One texture on a hundred meshes is uploaded once, and once more after Changed().
  • R2–R4 (OpenGL ES): tests with the recorder (one upload, UVs only for textured meshes, sampling as asked); the sample island with its textures drawn by Go UI’s cmd/mobile on the Android emulator.
  • R5 (3DS): the ETC1 encoder and the RGB565 and RGBA8 tiling tested against blocks and pixels worked out by hand; cmd/textures3ds in Azahar: cobbles in RGBA8, a roof and planks in RGB565, walls and ground in ETC1, 60 frames a second and 0.8 ms of citro3d’s processing with or without textures.
  • R6: a small .glb with an embedded PNG, and one with the picture beside it.
  • R7: see the optimization plan: a scene without textures costs the same; a textured frame allocates nothing.

Found and fixed on the way: the scene renderer allocated 59 times a frame (cgo pointers to matrices on the stack), now none outside the 3D labels; a first 3DS build checked every pixel for transparency on every draw.

Still to be reviewed by a person: everything (AI-written); the Metal renderer was written without a Mac and has never been compiled or run; nothing was tried on a real 3DS or phone. Left out: textures on the 3DS’s water and moving nodes, mipmaps on the 3DS, and the 3D labels’ five allocations a frame. Decisions 5 to 8 were built after the review: the deprecated pattern extras, the textures’ Gain, and go-engine’s feature/material-textures.
