Tutorial: an island with a lighthouse

In six short lessons you build one program, a step at a time: a hill rising out of the sea, a lighthouse whose lamp turns, a forest that sways in the wind, the sky by day and by night, labels over things, and trees you plant with a click. Each lesson adds a little to the program of the one before it and ends with something you can run.

Before you start, follow Get started: Go, a C compiler and Go Renderer cloned beside your module. Each lesson takes ten to twenty minutes.

Every lesson has the same shape, as in a quest: the idea in everyday words, the code, a checkpoint to know it works, a bonus with its solution folded, and an achievement to unlock. A final challenge waits at the end of lesson 6.

Lesson You learn You end with
1. Meshes and nodes Triangles, normals, materials, placing things with a transform A tower on a hill
2. Making things move The update function, children of a node, instancing a forest A lamp that turns, a forest
3. Light, sky and water The environment: sun, fog, sea, wind, clouds, the night, glowing materials An island in the sea, by day and by night
4. Labels and the overlay Labels over the world, the header, notices, free 2D drawing Names and messages on screen
5. Editing with the pointer viewer.Edit, the input state, finding the ground under the pointer Trees planted where you click
6. Scenes from files glTF, the renderer’s extras, embedding a scene in the program The same island, made in another tool

Every lesson closes with practice: a small change to try on your own, with a solution folded under it. The complete program is at the end of lesson 5.

💡 Captures. Every program built on viewer takes the viewer’s flags. -capture shot.png draws one frame into a picture and exits, which is how the pictures in these lessons were made; -time 3 draws it as if three seconds had passed, so two captures can be compared.
